# Persistent formation condition — v0.7.2

Each deployed or reserve unit group is now a persistent formation with a stable ID, health (0–100), experience (0–100), healing rate, native/trained terrain adaptations, supply rating, weaponry rating, battle count and completed training count. It retains the existing exact catalog personnel/equipment quantities until the formation is destroyed. Health is a game abstraction for combat effectiveness, not a count of individual injured soldiers. Destroyed groups are removed; recovery never resurrects them.

New records start at full health, normal supply and weaponry, and zero combat experience. Deployed formations know their initial territory's terrain; national reserves use their home terrain. Experience is earned by actual engagements (+12 per battle) or paid training. Existing saves receive additive condition records without resetting treasuries, troops or sessions; unavailable historical per-unit experience is not invented retroactively.

Chair combat power now considers health, experience, supply, weaponry and terrain familiarity alongside existing unit power, terrain defenses, readiness, fortifications and national advantages. At comparable strengths, a winning-side formation loses approximately 42–50 health and a losing-side formation approximately 55–63 health per engagement. Damage scales down when opposition is much weaker, so one small raiding group cannot wound an entire large army as though it faced an equal force. Typical unsupported formations therefore last two or three repeated monthly battles, rather than fighting indefinitely. A badly wounded formation can be destroyed sooner; a unit that rests, trains, withdraws or avoids engagement can survive longer. Retreat/capture and encirclement can also remove formations. This is accelerated board-game pacing, not a real-life combat model.

At month settlement, normal funded recovery is six health points; financial crisis reduces it to two, and low supply yields three. Supply and weaponry are normally good. Insufficient upkeep, an empty treasury, shortages of supplies or unavailable spares reduce condition. Experience and terrain familiarity do not vanish when a formation moves. Deployment, reserve return, remobilization, blockade patrols and surviving retreats transfer the same records instead of replacing wounded units with pristine ones. Active formation IDs must remain unique.

The Chair supports `training {target, terrain, budget, title}`. Courses require an owned site, a represented board terrain and an affordable $25–$1,000 budget. Experience gain is capped at 25 per course. Courses cover that site's formations and national reserves. Successfully completed funded training initiatives also award training once; suspended/resumed projects cannot repeatedly apply it. Supported terrain categories currently follow the board: urban, mountain and plains.

The condition system lives behind the existing five tabs, without additional visual meters. Own condition records are supplied to the Crisis Chair; classified enemy records are omitted from player projections. Private battle decisions report survivor condition. AI governments use their own condition in offensive selection and can fund readiness courses; they do not receive classified enemy health as player intelligence.

New rooms default to a 3,600-second monthly timer. The server must remain running for the timer to advance the world. Manual host advancement and shorter timers remain available. Existing rooms keep their saved timer; merely updating source does not change a running campaign's time limit.

The shared Gemini adapter retries HTTP 500/502/503/504 up to two times with short delays. Quota, key, model and validation failures are not blindly retried. Messages include the HTTP status but never provider response bodies or credentials. A failed Chair request remains deferred, with no fabricated local success. Provider tests use controlled transports; no new live key verification is claimed for this release.
